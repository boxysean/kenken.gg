About kenken.gg

kenken.gg was built by boxysean for everyone out there who wants to continue to sit on the couch, pick up their phone, and figure out the answer to the KenKen they just failed to solve.

How photo scanning works, explained like you're five

You take a photo of a KenKen. The computer has to turn that photo into a puzzle it can solve. It can't look at the whole thing at once the way you do, so it works in small steps, one after another, like following a recipe.

If any step goes wrong, everything after it goes wrong too: if the corners are off, the cages and clues will be too. That's why each step gets checked on its own (see the report card below).

Step 4 is the hard one: teaching the computer to read

Steps 1–3 follow fixed rules. Reading a clue is different: there are many fonts, and newspapers, books and printouts all write 2− or 12÷ a little differently. So instead of writing rules, we teach a small program, the reader, the way a child learns letters: with flash cards. Each card shows a picture of a character, with the right answer on the back.

+ → →

The reader only knows what its flash cards showed it. One trained only on neat practice cards gets confused by a real newspaper's font, much as you might be the first time you saw cursive. Real cards fix that, and those come from labelled photos.

Labelling a photo: making the answer key

A labelled photo is a photo plus an answer key: what a person says is really in it. Anyone can make one in the Label tab:

→ →

One answer key is useful twice. It's a test: run the photo through the five steps and compare each one with the key. And it's a source of real flash cards: every clue in the photo becomes a card with its answer already known.

The report card that only goes up

Every labelled photo is kept, and every change to the scanner is graded against all of them, one step at a time. The rule is simple: a grade can go up, but it can never go down. A change that improves one photo but breaks another isn't allowed in.
